## Your Core Responsibilities

These are the things you own. They don't all happen in week one, but know them now.

- **Account health monitoring** — Regularly review usage data, engagement signals, and support volume to catch risk early
- **Customer check-ins** — Run scheduled calls with accounts to review progress, surface issues, and reinforce value
- **Onboarding** — Guide new customers through their first weeks with the product
- **Escalation management** — When something goes wrong, you coordinate the response across support, product, and engineering
- **Renewal and expansion support** — Partner with sales when accounts are up for renewal or ready to grow
- **Internal feedback loop** — Bring customer insights back to the product and leadership teams

---

## What Success Looks Like

**In your first 30 days**, you know your accounts, you understand the product, and you've built relationships internally.

**In your first 60 days**, you're running your own customer calls, managing your book independently, and flagging risks before they escalate.

**In your first 90 days**, you're contributing to team processes, sharing customer feedback internally, and operating without much hand-holding.

Your manager will set specific targets with you. The above is a general picture.

## A Few Things Worth Knowing Early

Customers will test your patience sometimes. That's the job. Stay calm, stay curious, and ask questions before offering solutions.

You'll get asked things you don't know the answer to. Say so, and follow up. Customers respect honesty far more than false confidence.

Your relationships with internal teams — support, product, sales — matter as much as your relationships with customers. Invest in them.
