# Pricing Strategy Recommendation

## The Core Problem

$49/month flat is killing you. You're too expensive for small teams who'd compare you to Mixpanel, and you look cheap to enterprise buyers who'd expect to pay like Amplitude. You're stuck in the worst possible position — the middle.

---

## Recommended Model: Usage-Based Tiers

Three tiers anchored around team size and data volume.

**Starter — $0/month**
- Up to 1,000 monthly tracked users
- 2 seats
- 90-day data history
- Core reports only

This kills objections from small teams. They start free, grow into paying.

**Growth — $79/month**
- Up to 25,000 monthly tracked users
- 10 seats
- 12-month data history
- All standard features + exports

This is your volume tier. Price it above Mixpanel's comparable plan intentionally — you want to signal you're the better product, not the cheaper one.

**Pro — $299/month**
- Up to 150,000 monthly tracked users
- Unlimited seats
- Full data history
- Priority support + API access

**Enterprise — Custom**
- SSO, SLAs, dedicated onboarding, compliance docs
- Floor this at $1,500/month in conversations

---

## The Anchoring Logic

Showing Enterprise pricing (even as "custom") makes $299 feel reasonable. $79 feels like a steal by comparison. This is deliberate. Buyers scan all four tiers and anchor to the most expensive before evaluating the others.

---

## Annual Pricing

Offer 2 months free for annual payment. That's a 17% discount that costs you less than churn does. Put it front and center on the pricing page — don't hide it.

---

## What to Do With Existing $49 Customers

Don't grandfather them forever. Give them 90 days notice, migrate them to Growth at a locked $59/month for 12 months, then full price. Most will stay. The ones who leave were probably your lowest-value customers anyway.

---

## Three Things to Do This Week

1. **Audit your best 20 customers.** What do they have in common? That profile defines your Growth and Pro buyer personas, and those personas should be reflected in how you describe each tier.

2. **Add a value metric to your pricing page.** "Per tracked user" or "per seat" makes price increases feel logical and fair later. Flat pricing traps you.

3. **Talk to 5 prospects who didn't convert.** Price objections usually reveal which tier boundary is wrong. Fix the tiers before you launch, not after.

---

## Expected Outcome

Your average contract value should move from $49 to somewhere between $120–180 within 6 months as existing customers migrate and new customers self-select into higher tiers. Enterprise deals, even one or two per quarter, change the math entirely.
