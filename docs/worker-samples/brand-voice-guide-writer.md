# Acme Analytics Brand Voice Guide

**Prepared by 3rdShift**

---

## Who Acme Analytics Talks To

SaaS founders and operators are busy, skeptical, and sharp. They've seen every pitch. They don't need hand-holding and they don't have time for filler. They want to know what something does, why it matters, and what to do next. Acme Analytics earns their attention by respecting it.

---

## Voice Attributes

### 1. Sharp

Acme Analytics gets to the point. Every sentence earns its place. The writing is clear, specific, and confident — never vague, never padded. If something can be said in eight words instead of eighteen, say it in eight.

> *Sharp is about precision, not bluntness. It's the difference between a scalpel and a sledgehammer.*

---

### 2. Straight-Talking

No hedging. No corporate speak. Acme Analytics says what it means, means what it says, and doesn't dress up simple ideas in complicated language. The tone is confident without being arrogant. Direct without being cold.

> *Straight-talking is what makes founders trust you. They've been lied to by dashboards before.*

---

### 3. Warm Intelligence

Acme Analytics is smart — and it knows its audience is too. The writing never talks down. It also never tries to sound impressive by being complex. Smart people explain things clearly. Acme Analytics writes like the smartest person in the room who also happens to be good company.

> *Warm intelligence is not cheerleader energy. It's a colleague who genuinely knows their stuff and wants you to succeed.*

---

### 4. Grounded

Acme Analytics stays close to reality. Claims are specific. Examples are real. The writing avoids hype, superlatives, and promises that can't be backed up. When something is genuinely great, it's okay to say so — once, plainly, with proof nearby.

> *Grounded brands don't need to shout. The substance does the work.*

---

### 5. Quietly Confident

Acme Analytics doesn't beg for attention or pile on the exclamation points. It knows what it does and who it's for. The tone carries authority without demanding deference. It's the written equivalent of a firm handshake.

> *Confidence without arrogance. Certainty without smugness.*

---

## Channel Do's and Don'ts

---

### Website — Hero / Homepage

**Do:** Lead with what the product does. Be specific about who it's for. Trust that the audience will lean in if you give them something real.

**Don't:** Open with a question. Start with a tagline that could apply to any analytics tool. Use "We believe..." or "In a world where..."

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Unlock the power of your data and gain the visibility you need to make smarter decisions and drive growth at scale."*

**✅ After**

> *"Acme Analytics shows SaaS founders exactly what's moving revenue — and what isn't. No guesswork. No noise."*

---

### Website — Feature Copy

**Do:** Name the feature, say what it does in plain language, then say why that matters. One idea per paragraph.

**Don't:** Lead with the category name. Write feature specs that read like a changelog. Use passive voice.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Our real-time dashboard functionality leverages advanced data processing capabilities to surface actionable metrics."*

**✅ After**

> *"The live dashboard updates the moment your data does. So when a campaign goes live or a churn signal appears, you see it — before it's a problem."*

---

### Email — Marketing / Nurture

**Do:** Write a subject line that earns the open. Get to the point in the first sentence. Have one clear CTA. Sound like a person, not a broadcast.

**Don't:** Open with "We're excited to share..." Use multiple CTAs. Write more than 200 words for a nurture email unless there's a very good reason.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Subject: Exciting News from Acme Analytics!*
>
> *Hi [First Name], we're thrilled to announce a new feature that will revolutionize the way you interact with your data and empower your team to make better decisions..."*

**✅ After**

> *"Subject: Your churn signal was hiding in plain sight*
>
> *Most SaaS teams find out about churn after it happens. Acme Analytics flags the behavioral patterns before the cancellation email lands. Here's how it works →"*

---

### Email — Transactional / Product

**Do:** Be direct. Confirm what happened, what comes next, and who to contact if something's wrong. Plain language, no styling required.

**Don't:** Turn a confirmation email into a marketing opportunity. Use exclamation points. Write more than necessary.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"🎉 You're all set! Welcome to the Acme Analytics family! We're SO excited to have you on board and can't wait to help you leverage your data to achieve incredible growth!"*

**✅ After**

> *"You're in. Your workspace is ready at [link]. If anything looks off, reply here or reach out to support@acmeanalytics.com."*

---

### Social — LinkedIn

**Do:** Share a specific observation, data point, or insight. Write in short paragraphs. Let the idea do the work. Engage with comments like a person, not a brand account.

**Don't:** Use hashtag stacks. Post motivational content. Open with "Thrilled to announce..." Mirror every post in the caption as an image.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Excited to share that Acme Analytics has reached a major milestone! 🚀 We couldn't have done it without our incredible customers! Here's to empowering more teams to leverage data-driven insights! #SaaS #DataAnalytics #GrowthHacking #Startup #B2B"*

**✅ After**

> *"Most SaaS dashboards tell you what happened.*
>
> *The useful question is why it happened — and what's coming next.*
>
> *That's the gap we built Acme Analytics to close."*

---

### Social — Twitter/X

**Do:** One idea, said well. Use threads when an idea needs room. Engage in conversations. Be specific over general.

**Don't:** Live-tweet your own announcements. Use hollow affirmations. Post the same content you published on LinkedIn, just shorter.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Data is the key to unlocking your company's potential! 🔑 Make smarter decisions today with Acme Analytics! #Analytics #SaaS"*

**✅ After**

> *"The metric SaaS founders check the most is usually not the one causing the problem. That's the whole product."*

---

### Sales Outreach — Cold Email / LinkedIn DM

**Do:** Reference something specific. Be clear about what you do and why you're reaching out. Respect their time by being brief. Make the ask small.

**Don't:** Open with compliments. Use "I'll be brief" (and then aren't). Send an essay. Ask for a call before establishing any relevance.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Hi [Name], I hope this message finds you well! I came across your profile and was really impressed by what you're building at [Company]. I wanted to reach out because I think Acme Analytics could be a great fit to help you leverage your data and empower your team to drive growth. Would love to jump on a quick 30-minute call to explore synergies. Let me know!"*

**✅ After**

> *"Hi [Name] — Acme Analytics tracks the metrics SaaS operators actually make decisions from: revenue signals, churn indicators, activation rates.*
>
> *Worth a 20-minute look? Happy to show you how it maps to [Company's] stage."*

---

### In-Product Copy — Tooltips, Empty States, Error Messages

**Do:** Say exactly what the user needs to know or do. In empty states, explain what will appear here and how to get started. In errors, say what went wrong and what to do next.

**Don't:** Be cute or vague in moments of friction. Use error codes without translation. Over-apologize.

---

**❌ Before**

> *"Oops! Something went wrong on our end. We're sorry for the inconvenience! Please try again later."*

**✅ After**

> *"We couldn't load your dashboard. Try refreshing — if it keeps happening, contact support and we'll sort it."*

---

**❌ Before (Empty State)**

> *"No data yet! 🎉 Get started on your data journey by connecting your first integration!"*

**✅ After (Empty State)**

> *"Nothing here yet. Connect your first data source and your metrics will appear here automatically."*
