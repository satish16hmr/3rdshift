# Interview Question Set
## Senior Customer Success Manager | Acme Analytics

---

### QUESTION 1
**Tell me about a time you turned around a customer who was actively considering leaving.**

**What to listen for:**
- Specific actions taken, not vague platitudes
- How quickly they identified the real problem (often different from the stated one)
- Whether they involved internal teams or went it alone
- Long-term follow-through, not just a short-term fix

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Identified root cause, built a recovery plan, involved stakeholders, and can show the customer stayed and grew |
| 2 – Acceptable | Saved the account but the approach was reactive and mostly relationship-based |
| 1 – Weak | Vague story, unclear outcome, or the customer left anyway with no lessons drawn |

---

### QUESTION 2
**Describe a situation where you had to deliver bad news to a customer — a missed deadline, a product limitation, something they didn't want to hear. How did you handle it?**

**What to listen for:**
- Did they delay or act promptly?
- Did they come with a solution or just the problem?
- Tone: empathetic but direct, not defensive
- How the customer responded and what happened next

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Communicated early, framed clearly, brought alternatives, maintained trust |
| 2 – Acceptable | Delivered the news but struggled with the follow-through or customer reaction |
| 1 – Weak | Delayed telling the customer, blamed other teams, or had no plan attached to the conversation |

---

### QUESTION 3
**You're managing a book of business with more accounts than you can give equal attention to. Walk me through how you prioritize.**

**What to listen for:**
- A real system, not just "I go by gut"
- Whether they factor in revenue, health scores, growth potential, and risk
- How they make sure lower-priority accounts don't fall through completely
- Comfort with trade-offs and transparency about them

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Clear tiering methodology, uses data, proactive touchpoints even for lower-tier accounts, can explain the logic to leadership |
| 2 – Acceptable | Has some prioritization instinct but it's informal or inconsistent |
| 1 – Weak | No real system, treats all accounts the same, or neglects segments entirely |

---

### QUESTION 4
**Tell me about a time you spotted an expansion or upsell opportunity and drove it to a close. How did you know it was the right moment?**

**What to listen for:**
- They framed the expansion around customer value, not quota
- They read signals — usage data, business changes, conversations
- Collaboration with Sales if applicable
- Whether the customer actually expanded and stayed happy

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Data-informed, customer-value-led, coordinated internally, successful outcome |
| 2 – Acceptable | Identified the opportunity but needed prompting or leaned too heavily on the sales team |
| 1 – Weak | Can't point to a specific example or frames expansion as purely a sales activity |

---

### QUESTION 5
**Give me an example of a time a customer asked for something your product couldn't do. How did you manage that conversation and what did you do next?**

**What to listen for:**
- Honesty with the customer vs. overpromising
- How they surfaced the feedback internally (product team, roadmap process)
- Whether they found a workaround or alternative
- How the customer relationship held up afterward

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Honest with the customer, documented and escalated the feedback, offered alternatives, preserved the relationship |
| 2 – Acceptable | Handled the customer fine but didn't close the internal feedback loop |
| 1 – Weak | Overpromised, went quiet on the customer, or had no process for capturing product gaps |

---

### QUESTION 6
**Describe a time you had to influence something internally — a product decision, a resource allocation, a process change — on behalf of a customer.**

**What to listen for:**
- How they built a case (data, business impact, customer context)
- Who they had to convince and how they approached it
- Whether they were persistent without being aggressive
- The outcome for both the customer and the internal team

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Built a compelling case with data, navigated stakeholders well, achieved a result or got a clear decision with rationale |
| 2 – Acceptable | Advocated for the customer but without structure or struggled to gain traction internally |
| 1 – Weak | Didn't advocate, gave up quickly, or had no example of cross-functional influence |

---

### QUESTION 7
**You've just taken over a book of accounts from a CSM who left. Several customers are upset about the transition. What do you do in the first 30 days?**

**What to listen for:**
- Speed of outreach and how they triage
- How they get up to speed without burdening the customer
- Whether they listen before they act
- Concrete steps, not just "I'd build relationships"

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Immediate personal outreach, reads existing notes and data first, sets clear expectations, identifies at-risk accounts quickly |
| 2 – Acceptable | Sound instincts but the plan is surface-level or missing urgency |
| 1 – Weak | Generic answer, no real plan, waits for customers to come to them |

---

### QUESTION 8
**Tell me about the most complex customer situation you've managed — multiple stakeholders, competing priorities, high stakes. How did you keep it together?**

**What to listen for:**
- Stakeholder mapping and communication strategy
- How they managed competing agendas without taking sides
- Whether they stayed calm and structured under pressure
- What the resolution looked like

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Clear picture of the complexity, organized approach, kept all parties informed, resolved or significantly improved the situation |
| 2 – Acceptable | Managed the situation but it was mostly reactive and relationship-dependent |
| 1 – Weak | Can't articulate a truly complex situation or handled it by avoiding conflict |

---

### QUESTION 9
**Acme Analytics sells to data and analytics teams. If a customer's team isn't adopting the platform the way they said they would, what do you do?**

**What to listen for:**
- They don't just chase usage metrics — they investigate why
- Understanding of common adoption blockers (technical, organizational, motivational)
- Whether they involve the right people on the customer side (champions, executives)
- Proactive intervention before it becomes a churn risk

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Diagnoses root cause, addresses it at the right level, builds an adoption plan with the customer, tracks progress |
| 2 – Acceptable | Recognizes the problem and takes action but the approach is surface-level (more training, more check-ins) |
| 1 – Weak | Waits for the customer to raise it or treats it as purely a product or IT problem |

---

### QUESTION 10
**Tell me about a time you disagreed with how your company handled a customer situation. What did you do?**

**What to listen for:**
- They advocated for the customer professionally, not just complained
- How they balanced loyalty to their employer with doing right by the customer
- Whether they escalated appropriately and through the right channels
- Maturity in how they talk about it now — no bitterness, no vagueness

**Scoring Rubric:**
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 3 – Strong | Raised the concern clearly, proposed an alternative, accepted the final decision gracefully, learned something from it |
| 2 – Acceptable | Spoke up but without a clear path forward, or the outcome wasn't great |
| 1 – Weak | Either stayed silent and did nothing or went around proper channels; or can't think of an example |
