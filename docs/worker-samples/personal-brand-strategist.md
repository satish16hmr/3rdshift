There are two ways to misuse data.

**DAY 6 — Saturday** 📊
*Topic: CAC vs. CAC Payback Period — what most founders misunderstand*
*Format: Educational explainer*

---

A lot of SaaS founders track CAC. Fewer track CAC Payback Period. That's a problem.

Here's the difference and why it matters.

**CAC** (Customer Acquisition Cost) tells you how much you spend to acquire one customer. Useful.

**CAC Payback Period** tells you how many months it takes to recover that acquisition cost from the revenue that customer generates. More useful.

Why? Because CAC in isolation tells you nothing about sustainability.

Example:
Company A: CAC = $500. Monthly revenue per customer = $50.
CAC Payback = 10 months. Fine.

Company B: CAC = $500. Monthly revenue per customer = $25.
CAC Payback = 20 months. Potentially a cash flow problem.

Same CAC. Very different businesses.

For early-stage SaaS (under $5M ARR), I generally want to see CAC Payback under 18 months.
For growth-stage SaaS with strong NRR, you have more room — some businesses run 24-36 months payback intentionally.

But you can't make that judgment if you're only watching CAC.

Know both numbers. Build both into your dashboard.

At $1M ARR, you've proven people will pay. Now the question is: can you build a machine?

Here are the 5 metrics I focus on at this stage. In priority order.

**1. Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**
Your existing customers should be expanding. If NRR is below 100%, you're losing ground — even if you're adding new customers. Benchmark: 100%+ is stable. 110%+ is strong. 120%+ is exceptional.

**2. Activation Rate**
What percentage of signups reach your core "aha moment"? If this is below 40%, your onboarding is leaking value. Fix this before scaling acquisition.

**3. CAC Payback Period**
Covered this last week. Under 18 months at this stage. No exceptions if you're capital-constrained.

**4. Monthly Churn Rate**
Keep it under 2% monthly at this stage. That's ~22% annual. High, but survivable. Above 3% monthly and growth becomes very hard to sustain.

**5. Lead Velocity Rate (LVR)**
Month-over-month growth in qualified leads. LVR is a leading indicator of future revenue. MRR is a lagging indicator. Watch both, but LVR tells you where you're headed.

None of these require fancy tooling. A spreadsheet and discipline will get you started.

Save this. Come back to it monthly.
