Both platforms give you a team of AI members you can put to work across your business, and both deliver finished work, not just rough drafts. The difference comes down to two other things: how specialized each AI member is, and what the platform actually costs you once you're using it every day. ## What Both Platforms Actually Do

Before getting into the differences, give credit where it's due.

Sintra gives you a roster of AI personas you can assign to different functions, from marketing to operations to customer support. Each persona has a distinct personality, a defined role, and a consistent voice. The persona builder is genuinely good, and Sintra's members produce finished deliverables, not just drafts you have to rebuild. If your priority is brand consistency and broad role coverage from one roster, Sintra delivers that.

3rdShift takes a different bet. The platform runs [70+ AI team members](/workers) across text, audio, and video, each built as a specialist inside a specific discipline. The goal is depth and delivery: a finished asset, tuned to its category's standard, published directly into your tools.

Both platforms are positioned at small business owners and founders who are tired of paying freelancers $1,500 a month for work that shows up five days late. Same customer. The real question is specialization and cost.

## The Two Differences That Actually Matter

### Specialization

Sintra's members are broad personas. One persona covers a wide function, which is great for coverage but means the output in any single discipline is a generalist's version of it.

3rdShift's members are specialists. Ava writes articles. Oscar writes newsletters. Quinn writes landing page copy. Case handles X (Twitter). Jules handles scripts. Sam is a hook specialist with a library of 200+ proven hook patterns. Each one is built for a specific deliverable, so the output is tuned to that category, not approximated across ten.

The trade-off is real: Sintra's breadth-first model covers more roles with fewer members. 3rdShift's depth-first model produces sharper output in each category. If you want one persona that does a bit of everything, Sintra fits. If you want the best possible newsletter, article, or landing page, a specialist wins.

### Pricing and the Credit Trap

Most buyers don't see this difference until the bill arrives.

Sintra runs on credits. Users often report that chatting with the AI employees eats those credits, which forces them to buy top-ups. The advertised price and the real monthly cost end up being different numbers, because the meter is running even when you're just talking to your team to get a task right.

3rdShift is flat. Each plan comes with a clear task allowance, and chatting with your team members is completely free and included on every plan. No credits drain while you talk to them, no surprise top-ups, and the price on the page is the price you pay.

For a founder running lean, that predictability matters as much as the output. A cheap-looking plan that climbs with every conversation isn't actually cheap.

### Integration Surface

Worth a direct mention because it's a structural 3rdShift edge.

3rdShift team members create and deliver through live integrations. Your newsletter member connects to your email platform. Your article writer connects to your CMS. Your social members connect to your scheduling tools. The work lands where it lives.

Sintra delivers finished output too, but where that output lands and how many of your tools it connects to natively is worth asking them directly, since it varies. If your workflow lives in Notion, Webflow, ConvertKit, or similar, ask both platforms the integration question before you commit.
